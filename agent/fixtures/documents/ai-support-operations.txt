AI Support Operations Pilot - Internal Review

Overview

Northstar Support ran a six-week pilot to evaluate whether a local AI assistant
could reduce repetitive work for the technical support team. The pilot involved
18 support specialists across the billing and account-access queues. The system
was intentionally advisory: specialists reviewed every suggested response before
sending it to a customer.

Pilot scope

The assistant handled three workflows. It classified incoming requests, drafted
first responses, and retrieved relevant paragraphs from the internal support
handbook. It could not send messages, issue refunds, change customer accounts, or
close tickets. Those actions remained under human control.

During the pilot, the team processed 1,240 tickets with assistance enabled. The
assistant completed its requested workflow for 91 percent of those tickets. The
median assisted handling time was 6 minutes, compared with a 9-minute baseline
measured during the four weeks before the pilot. The median AI execution itself
took 38 seconds. Specialists accepted a draft without substantial editing in 62
percent of successful runs.

Operational findings

Account-access requests produced the strongest results because the resolution
steps were well documented. Billing disputes were less reliable when a ticket
referred to an unusual contract or an older pricing plan. In those cases,
specialists frequently ignored the draft and searched the handbook manually.

The assistant used an average of 4,800 tokens per completed ticket. Five percent
of runs exceeded 12,000 tokens, usually after repeated handbook searches. The
pilot configuration allowed three retries, but most successful runs used none.
The operations team observed 27 runs that reached the retry limit. Nineteen of
those runs were associated with incomplete or contradictory ticket descriptions.

Safety and privacy

Customer names and email addresses were removed before requests reached the
model. Ticket text and model output were retained for seven days in the pilot
environment for debugging. Access was limited to the support platform team.
The security review found no evidence that the model could perform account
changes, but it recommended testing prompt-injection attempts before a broader
rollout.

Open questions and risks

The pilot did not measure customer satisfaction, long-term resolution rate, or
the number of tickets reopened after an assisted response. It also did not test
languages other than English. The baseline handling-time comparison may be
affected by seasonal changes in ticket volume. No decision has been made about
whether seven-day retention is appropriate for production.

Proposed next step

The support platform team recommends a second pilot limited to account-access
requests. The next phase should add prompt-injection tests, track reopened
tickets, measure customer satisfaction, and set a lower retry limit for repeated
handbook searches. Billing workflows should remain advisory until contract and
legacy-pricing documentation is improved.
